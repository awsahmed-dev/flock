01 · Overview

A travel planner that celebrates the group, not just the individual.

Most travel apps are designed for a solo planner who relays decisions to everyone else. Paxawa starts from a different premise — the group is the user. Every screen accepts that planning is a collective act, and shapes itself around that.

02 · The problem

Group travel breaks down where the tools end.

Talk to anyone who has organised a trip for four or more people and they'll describe the same arc: excitement → spreadsheet → group chat → silence → resentment. The Discord-of-friends becomes a search engine for "who said yes to the hotel?" by week two.

The product wasn't missing. It was scattered across WhatsApp, Google Sheets, Splitwise, Notion, and someone's Notes app. Each one solved a sliver. None of them spoke to the rest.

Decisions get lost in chat
The vote on which Airbnb to book sits at message #847. Nobody can find it. The host re-books and someone gets the wrong dates.
Money math doesn't match memory
Three currencies, four payers, partial settles. The spreadsheet is right. The vibe says someone is owed more. Post-trip "Venmo me ¥3,200" tanks the friendship.
No one owns the plan
The one friend with a spreadsheet becomes the on-call coordinator. By day three of the trip they're exhausted, mildly resentful, and the group is asking what's next.
"The fix wasn't a better spreadsheet. It was making the spreadsheet disappear — so the question 'who decided what?' has the same answer for everyone in the trip." Design premise · early note
03 · Home

All trips. One glance.

04 · Planning

A map-first plan, not another list.

The map is the surface. Mapbox basemap, English labels, places pinned per-day. The route lines connect the day's points in order, drawn from Mapbox Directions so they follow real roads — not as-the-crow-flies.

Tap a day pill (D1, D2, D3…) and the rest dim. The right rail shows the day's items in sequence. The Add place search hits the Foursquare POI database first — so "KLCC" returns the actual tower, not a same-named street six countries away.

05 · Decide

A vote is a question with a deadline.

Open a vote, pick options, set a deadline. Anyone in the trip casts a tap. The winning option fills its bar gradually, so the result tells a story rather than barking a number.

When a vote closes, the winning option auto-adds to the itinerary on the relevant day. Decision and plan stay in lock-step — no copy-paste, no "I already added it" confusion.

The bar tells the story.

Each option's progress bar fills proportional to votes received. "YOUR VOTE" tag sits inside the row you picked, so changing your mind is one tap, not a hidden setting.

06 · Money

Eight currencies. One source of truth.

A trip can mix yen, ringgit, riyal, and dollars in a single afternoon. The naïve approach — sum the raw numbers — produces nonsense like "USD 880" on a trip that actually spent USD 130.

Paxawa pulls live FX rates from open.er-api.com on a stale-while-revalidate cache, then converts every row into the trip's base currency before adding it up. Each row also keeps its original — so the activity feed shows "JPY 380 ≈ USD 2.38", not a flattened number that loses context.

07 · Chat

The group chat that knows what the group is doing.

Most "trip chat" features are dead air with a different background. Paxawa's chat knows about the trip — it logs expenses as cards, posts vote results, and surfaces pre-trip nudges seven days out.

The composer has shortcuts: Expense, Vote, More. Type intent, skip nav.

7.1→Chat · AI nudges · composer shortcuts
SCENARIO 01
Pre-trip nudges
A cron posts a milestone message 30 / 14 / 7 / 1 days out. The 7-day nudge says "confirm flights and book any activities you're planning. Make sure everyone has their passport and travel insurance sorted."
SCENARIO 02
Expense logged · auto-card
When anyone logs an expense, an inline card appears in chat: "💸 Burger — JPY 800". Tap the card to open the expense detail sheet. The discussion happens around the card, not in a separate tab.
SCENARIO 03
Smart chip → vote
Type "let's vote where to eat tonight" and a "Vote?" smart chip appears above the composer. Tap it — the create-vote dialog opens with the question pre-filled from your sentence.
SCENARIO 04
Vote closed · result card
When a vote closes, the result posts to chat as a small card: "Beachfront wins — added to Day 2." No need to refresh the votes tab. Everyone sees it in the same scroll.
SCENARIO 05
Reactions on cards
Long-press any card (expense, vote, place) to drop a reaction. The reaction set is short: 👍 ❤️ 😂 ✈️ 🌶️. The fewer the choices, the less the deliberation.
SCENARIO 06
Day-zero check-in
The morning the trip starts, AI posts "Day 1 starts today. Here's where you're headed first: [first itinerary item]." It's a thread-opener, not a chore. The group takes it from there.
08 · Pack

Defaults are design.

Every new trip starts with a 20-item packing list seeded by the destination. Toiletries, documents, tech, medical, clothes. A Malaysia trip auto-includes insect repellent and a cooling towel. A ski trip would include thermal layers.

The activation gate flipped from "I'll get to it" to "let me uncheck what I don't need." Same item count, opposite emotional cost.

09 · Notifications

A bell that respects you.

Four channels: in-app bell, transactional email, push, daily digest. Each ships on, each is one toggle to mute. The settings page is the entire surface — no menu diving.

The bell itself sits in every trip header and on the dashboard. Opening it marks-all-read optimistically; the badge clears before the POST round-trip finishes.

Preferences

Four toggles. No menu diving.

The notification settings page is intentionally one screen. Four rows, each one a toggle and a one-line explanation of what flipping it off actually means.

Daily digest is a quieter alternative to per-event email — one summary, once a day, only if you have unread events.

10 · The rest of the system

Surfaces that earn their place.

A trip is more than five tabs. The supporting screens — Crew, Settings, Discover, Create — each carry one job and stay out of the way the rest of the time.

Crew · members

Who's coming. What they owe.

The Crew page lists every member with their role, per-member personal budget, and their net balance in trip currency. The owner has one extra control: change role / remove.

Invite-link sits at the top — one tap to copy, one to share via system share sheet.

11 · Process

Ship, audit, repeat.

Fifteen build iterations, each scoped to a clear theme. Every cycle ended with a tester walk-through that surfaced the next batch.

The biggest unlocks weren't features — they were the audits that caught the gaps between what shipped and what users actually trusted.

A1 – A4

Foundations & landing

Brand, landing page, legal, waitlist, in-app feedback widget. Got the shape of the product on paper before code.

B1 – B3

Core loops

Performance triage, expense bug sweep, budget v2 with per-member caps, role system (owner/member), and the map-first itinerary view.

B4 – B7

UI rebalance

Hero compaction, Money page rebuild around a single hero, Plan page rebuild on Mapbox + Foursquare, day polylines that follow real roads via Directions API.

B8 – B11

Audit cycles

Full UX/UI audit. Dialog overflow → BottomSheet on mobile. FX provider swap when exchangerate.host died quietly. Dashboard metrics, button positions, drag-to-expand sheets.

B12

Tester-driven fixes

Eight specific fixes from a real-trip walkthrough: Mapbox English labels, doc descriptions, receipt upload, FX projection, place-button placement, auto-seeded packing, Pack tab rename, full detective audit walk in MYR ↔ SAR.

B13

Notifications

In-app bell + dropdown, per-channel preferences (in-app, email, push, digest), trip-shell + dashboard bells, daily digest cron.

B14

Launch hardening

Timezone-safe calendar date parser, CRON_SECRET, smoke checks across votes / settle / AI plan / share. Ready for the test event.

B15

Case study

12 · Outcome

What I'd keep, what I'd watch.

The product holds together as a single thing. A trip isn't five apps stapled to a logo — it's one calm shape with five entry points.

The audits did more for trust than any one feature. Every time the team did a real walkthrough in a non-base currency, we caught something the unit tests couldn't.

01
Audits beat unit tests
The Money card on the dashboard summed raw numbers without converting — invisible to unit tests, instantly broken to a tester who spent in three currencies.
02
Defaults are design
Auto-seeding 20 packing items at trip creation moved the activation gate from "I'll get to it" to "let me uncheck what I don't need." Same item count, opposite emotional cost.
03
Show the conversion
When money is in a foreign currency, never hide the FX math. Show both numbers, every time. Instant trust on a multi-currency trip is the difference between using the app and screenshotting it into Notes.
